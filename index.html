01

Research before shortcuts

We gather available references, photographs, and technical material to understand the aircraft’s layout and behaviour. Research helps guide decisions; it doesn’t mean every real-world system is already implemented.

02

Shape and flight first

The first foundations are the exterior model, proportions, control surfaces, landing gear, and a flight model that can be tested and refined in X-Plane 12.

03

Build systems with purpose

As the project matures, cockpit indications and aircraft behaviour should respond to simulated states wherever practical, rather than being only decorative.

04

Test, adjust, repeat

Handling, visibility, animations, and system behaviour all need repeated checks. Development will be iterative, and features will be described honestly as they become available.

Chapter 01 · The flight deck

A cockpit that feels alive.

The flight deck should be more than a collection of screens and switches. The aim is to build a cockpit where the key indications make sense together and respond to what the aircraft is doing.

  • Readable flight and navigation displays
  • Standby instruments and essential indications
  • Switches and controls developed progressively
  • Careful attention to layout, scale, and lighting

These are development goals, not a claim that every item is already complete.

Chapter 02 · Aircraft systems

Systems with a reason to exist.

Electrical power, engines, fuel, warnings, and aircraft controls should work together as parts of one simulated aircraft. We’ll prioritise core behaviour first, then expand the depth as the project allows.

  • Engine indications and start-up behaviour
  • Electrical and essential aircraft services
  • Warnings and system states tied to simulated conditions
  • Progressive testing in normal and abnormal situations

Real-aircraft references guide the work, while the depth of each feature depends on available documentation and development time.
